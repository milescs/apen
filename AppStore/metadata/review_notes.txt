Apen is a voice dictation (speech-to-text) app: a voice input method, which is an accessibility use for people who can't or prefer not to type.

How to test:
1. Launch Apen. It lives in the menu bar (an "A" with a leaf) and opens a welcome window.
2. Click Allow Microphone, then Download next to "Speech model" (about 600 MB from Hugging Face, one time).
3. Click into any text field (for example in TextEdit), press Option-Space, say a sentence, and press Option-Space again. The text is pasted into the field.

Why Accessibility: Apen uses it only to press Command-V once, inserting the text the user just dictated into the field they are typing in, as a keyboard would. It does not read or control other apps. Without the permission Apen copies the text to the clipboard instead and remains fully functional.

Downloads: the speech model and the optional cleanup model are machine-learning weights (data, not code), downloaded only when the user clicks Download, with the size shown first. All processing happens on the Mac; no user data is sent anywhere. No account or sign-in is needed.
